Choosing a grade

Fe 500 vs Fe 500D vs Fe 550D

The "D" stands for ductility — a D-grade bar bends further before it fails, which is what matters in a seismic zone. The higher number is more strength. Here is what IS 1786 requires of each.

GradeMin yield strengthMin tensile strengthMin elongationTypically used for
Fe 500500 N/mm²545 N/mm²12%General residential and commercial RCC work
Fe 500D500 N/mm²565 N/mm²16%Same strength, more ductility — the common choice for seismic zones
Fe 550550 N/mm²585 N/mm²10%Higher strength where the design calls for it
Fe 550D550 N/mm²600 N/mm²14.5%High strength with ductility — heavier structures

Figures are the minimums laid down in IS 1786. Actual values on any given batch appear on its mill test certificate, which we supply on request. If you are unsure which grade your drawing specifies, send it over and we'll confirm before you order.

Know your material

What makes a TMT bar different

TMT stands for thermo-mechanically treated. The bar is quenched from rolling heat so the outer skin hardens, then allowed to self-temper so the core stays soft. That combination is the whole point.

  • Strength where it is needed — the hardened outer layer carries the load, so a thinner bar does the work of a heavier plain one.
  • Ductility in the core — the soft centre lets the bar bend and absorb movement rather than snapping. This is what matters in an earthquake.
  • Bends without cracking — important on site, where bars are bent to shape rather than used straight.
  • Better bond with concrete — the rib pattern rolled into the surface is what grips the concrete around it.
